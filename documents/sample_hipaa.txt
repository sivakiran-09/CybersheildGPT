HIPAA Breach Notification Rule

The HIPAA Breach Notification Rule requires covered entities to notify affected individuals
following the discovery of a breach of unsecured protected health information (PHI). Notification
must generally occur within 60 days of discovery. If a breach affects 500 or more individuals in a
single jurisdiction, the covered entity must also notify the Department of Health and Human Services
and, in many cases, prominent media outlets in that jurisdiction.

A breach is defined as the acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI in a manner not permitted
under the Privacy Rule, which compromises the security or privacy of the PHI. Certain exceptions
apply, including unintentional access by a workforce member acting in good faith and within the
scope of authority.

Minimum Necessary Standard

Covered entities and their workforce members must limit PHI access, use, and disclosure to the
minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose. Bulk or wholesale access to patient records
that is not tied to a specific treatment, payment, or operations need may itself constitute a
violation, independent of any subsequent data exfiltration.

Incident Response Expectations

Organizations are expected to have a documented incident response plan that includes identification,
containment, eradication, recovery, and post-incident review. For incidents involving PHI, the
response plan should integrate directly with breach-notification timelines and the organization's
privacy officer.
