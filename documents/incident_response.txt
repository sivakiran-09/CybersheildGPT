Incident Response Plan

An effective incident response plan follows six phases: preparation, identification,
containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons learned.

Preparation involves maintaining an up-to-date asset inventory, defined escalation contacts,
and a tested communication plan. Identification requires monitoring tools capable of detecting
anomalous behavior, such as unusual login patterns or large data transfers.

During containment, responders should isolate affected systems without destroying evidence.
Short-term containment might involve network segmentation, while long-term containment could
include patching vulnerabilities or rebuilding compromised systems.

Eradication removes the root cause, such as malware or an attacker's persistence mechanism.
Recovery restores systems to normal operation with enhanced monitoring to detect any recurrence.

The lessons-learned phase should occur within two weeks of incident closure and should update
the incident response plan itself based on gaps identified during the response.
