Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) Policy

All accounts with administrative privileges, remote access capability, or access to systems
containing PHI must be protected by multi-factor authentication. This includes EHR
administrator accounts, VPN access, and cloud management consoles.

Acceptable MFA methods include authenticator apps and hardware security keys. SMS-based
verification is discouraged due to susceptibility to SIM-swapping attacks, and should only be
used as a fallback when no stronger method is available.

MFA fatigue attacks, where an attacker repeatedly triggers push notifications hoping the user
approves one by mistake, should be mitigated through number-matching MFA prompts rather than
simple approve/deny notifications.

Accounts detected with a high volume of failed login attempts followed by a successful login
from a new device or location should be flagged for immediate review, even when MFA was
technically satisfied, since MFA fatigue and social engineering can bypass the control.
