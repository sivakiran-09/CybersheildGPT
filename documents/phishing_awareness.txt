Phishing Awareness and Prevention

Phishing remains the most common initial access vector for healthcare breaches. Attackers often
impersonate IT support, vendors, or internal executives to trick staff into revealing
credentials or executing malicious attachments.

Warning signs include urgent or threatening language, requests to bypass normal approval
processes, mismatched sender domains, and unexpected attachments or links. Staff should be
trained to verify unusual requests through a separate communication channel rather than
replying directly to the suspicious message.

Simulated phishing campaigns should be run quarterly, with additional targeted training for
staff who repeatedly fall for simulations. Clinical staff with access to patient records are a
particularly high-value target and should receive role-specific training examples relevant to
healthcare-themed phishing lures.

Any credential entered into a suspected phishing page should be treated as compromised
immediately, triggering a forced password reset and review of recent account activity for signs
of unauthorized access.
